Use this when someone asks how a product, service or offer compares with its alternatives.

1. Agree the set: at most five alternatives, including doing nothing or doing it by hand. Ask if the set is unclear.
2. Pick the four to six points a buyer decides on (price, what is included, how long it takes to start, where it falls short), not a feature list.
3. Fill each point from each alternative's own published material first, then from independent reviews. Note the date of every fact; prices and plans change.
4. Mark anything you could not confirm as unknown rather than filling it with a guess.
5. Say where each alternative is genuinely better. A scan that finds no strengths in anyone else is not believed.

Not for: a single fact about one product. Look that up and answer it.
Return: a table of alternatives by decision points, then three lines on where we win, where we lose, and what to watch. Sources with dates at the end.
Approval: none needed to research public material. Required before signing up for a trial or paying for access.
