Use this when a prospect has asked for a proposal, a quote or a statement of work.

1. Open with their problem in their own words, from the notes or the thread, and what success looks like to them. If you cannot say it, ask before writing.
2. Describe the approach in plain steps: what will be done, by whom, and when. Name what is not included.
3. Price it from the figures the person gave you. Never invent a price, a discount or a term.
4. List the assumptions the price depends on, so a change in scope is a conversation, not an argument.
5. End with the next step and the date the offer stands until.
6. Keep it short enough to read in five minutes. Detail goes in an appendix.

Not for: a quick price question in a thread. Answer it there with the figure and its conditions.
Return: the proposal as a document with headed sections, and a list of anything you had to assume.
Approval: always required before it is sent. Prices, discounts and terms are the person's to set.
