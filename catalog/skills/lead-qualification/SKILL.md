Use this when there is a list of prospects or one new lead, and someone needs to know which are worth pursuing.

1. Agree the profile first: the kind of organisation or person who gets the most from what is being sold, and the signs that say so. Ask if it is not written down.
2. For each lead, look for evidence of need (a problem the offer solves, visible in their own words or actions), ability to buy (size, budget, who decides), and timing (a reason to act now).
3. Score each as strong, possible or poor, with the one fact that decided it. No fact, no score: mark it unknown.
4. Note anything that rules a lead out: a competitor relationship, a policy, a recent no.
5. Use public information only. Do not guess at private details or contact anyone.

Not for: writing the outreach itself. Qualify first, then draft for the strong ones.
Return: strong leads first, each with the deciding fact and a suggested angle, then possible, then poor with the reason in a few words.
Approval: none needed to research. Contacting anyone needs approval.
