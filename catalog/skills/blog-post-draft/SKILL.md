Use this when there is a topic, a brief or an outline and someone wants a full draft of an article or a post.

1. Write the one sentence the reader should remember. If the brief has more than one, ask which, or split it into two pieces.
2. Open with the problem or the moment the reader recognises, in two or three sentences. No throat clearing, no "in today's world".
3. Make the point, then back it with something concrete: an example, a number with its source, a short story, a before and after.
4. Use headings only if it runs past about 600 words. Keep paragraphs to three or four lines.
5. End with what the reader can do now. No summary of what they just read.
6. Match the voice of the person's earlier pieces when there are any. Mark every fact that still needs a source with [source?].

Not for: a social post, or editing a draft someone already wrote. Edit that draft rather than starting again.
Return: a title, the draft, and the list of facts marked for checking.
Approval: required before it is published.
