Use this when there are notes or transcripts from several conversations with users or customers, and someone wants to know what they add up to.

1. Read every interview before grouping anything.
2. Pull out what people did and what happened to them, above what they said they would like. Past behaviour predicts better than wishes.
3. Group into patterns. A pattern needs at least three people; anything less is a note, not a finding.
4. For each pattern, give how many people it covers, two short quotes, and what it suggests.
5. Keep the surprises and the disagreements. They are often the most useful part.
6. Refer to people by a label (P1, P2), never by name, and leave out anything that would identify them.

Not for: one interview somebody wants summarised. Summarise that interview.
Return: the patterns, strongest first, each with its count, quotes and implication, then the surprises.
Approval: none needed. Sharing quotes outside the team needs approval.
