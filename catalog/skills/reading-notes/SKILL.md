Use this when someone hands over a long document (a report, a contract, a paper, a thread) and wants to know what is in it without reading it all.

1. Read the whole thing before writing anything. Summaries made while reading overweight the beginning.
2. Pull out, in order: what it decides or asks for, the three to five claims it rests on, the numbers that matter, and the dates or deadlines in it.
3. Note what it does not say that a careful reader would expect it to: a missing cost, an unstated assumption, a party with no say.
4. Quote exact wording for anything that commits someone to something. Paraphrase everything else.
5. Give a location (page, section or heading) for every point so it can be checked.

Not for: one question about the document. Find the answer and quote it.
Return: one sentence on what the document is for, then decisions, claims, numbers, dates and gaps as short lists. Under 300 words for anything under fifty pages.
Approval: none needed, this is read-only.
