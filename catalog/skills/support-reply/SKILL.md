Use this when a customer has written in and a reply needs drafting.

1. Read the whole message and any earlier messages in the thread. Find the one thing they actually want: an answer, a fix, a refund, or to be heard.
2. Check the facts of their case before answering: their plan, what they did, what the product does. Never promise something you have not confirmed is possible.
3. Open with the answer or the next step, not with thanks or an apology formula. Apologise once, specifically, only if something went wrong on our side.
4. If you need something from them, ask for it in one clear question, and say why.
5. Say what happens next and when, in their terms ("you will get an email when it is fixed", not "we have escalated").
6. Match their language and level. No jargon, no internal names, no policy lectures.

Not for: an angry message that threatens legal action, a security report or a payment dispute. Hand those to the lead with a one line summary.
Return: the reply, ready to send, then one line on anything you could not confirm.
Approval: required before any reply is sent, and before any refund, credit or exception is offered.
